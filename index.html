A one-time-purchase tool ($19) that runs entirely in your browser: drop in your resume PDF, paste the job description, and get the machine-readable text order, 12 layout risk checks, and JD keyword coverage with a 0–100 score. The MVP is built and tested; it is not on sale yet. This page answers one question: at $19 one-time, is it worth launching.

Why validate first

  • The incumbent, Jobscan, charges $49.95/month; its free tier caps at 5 scans per month — run out and you hit a paywall. Other free web checkers require uploading your resume to their servers.
  • This tool parses locally in your browser: your resume file never leaves your machine, and there is no server, no tracking, no analytics. One-time $19 instead of a subscription targets the occasional job seeker — someone who job-hunts once or twice a year and does not want another monthly bill.
  • Honest limitation, stated up front: the checks are heuristic rules and have not been validated against real ATS systems. They estimate formatting risk; they do not guarantee any real system's verdict. If you need a certified ATS verdict, this tool is not it.

What it checks (built, tested, not yet for sale)

  • Machine-readable text order view — what a parser actually sees when it reads your PDF
  • 12 layout risk checks, graded pass / warn / fail with severity P0 (critical) / P1 (likely problem) / P2 (minor): multi-column layout, table-like alignment, contact info in the header/footer zone, non-standard section headings, too many fonts, low readable-text coverage / scanned pages, PDF reading-order mismatch, decorative characters, inconsistent date formats, unexpanded abbreviations, images combined with little text, missing machine-readable contact info
  • JD keyword coverage — paste the job description to see which keywords appear and which are missing; 0–100 score with severity-weighted deductions

Tested in headless Chromium on two synthetic resumes: a clean single-column resume scored 91 ("Good"); a two-column, table-heavy resume with header contact info scored 63 ("Needs work") with the expected checks firing. Two scan-related checks are implemented but unverified by synthetic fixtures — disclosed in the tool itself.
